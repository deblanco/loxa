# The slideshow formulas

Reverse-engineered from three TikTok accounts running the same machine for an
app called Albo: `tips.with.alex`, `leah.tiktok.hoarder`, `emily_internet_hoarder`.
Everything below is measured off their posts, then ported to Loxa.

This is a distribution format, not a design system. Where it contradicts
`design-system/`, it wins — see **The carve-out** at the bottom.

---

## 0. What the format actually is

A TikTok **Photo carousel** of 5–8 slides. Never a video. Never a face talking.

The whole post is a *list of outcomes*. The app is an ingredient inside the
list, never the subject of it. Nobody in these posts is selling anything, which
is exactly why they work.

The three accounts, measured:

| handle | following | followers | likes | likes ÷ followers |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `tips.with.alex` | 50 | 3,045 | 284.8K | 93× |
| `leah.tiktok.hoarder` | 44 | 21.7K | 2M | 92× |
| `emily_internet_hoarder` | 65 | 5,908 | 585.8K | 99× |

Three accounts, three near-identical multiples. That is not an audience — that
is reach without an audience. Follower count is not the machine. Volume plus
search is the machine.

## 1. The persona formula

```
[first name] + [self-deprecating collector noun]
```

`leah.tiktok.hoarder` · `emily_internet_hoarder` · `tips.with.alex`

Bios, verbatim:

> i save way too many videos so i try them instead

> just a girl with too many saves 🔍

The bio confesses a **flaw that implies labour**. She has already done the
sorting you have not done. That is the entire trust mechanism, and it takes
nine words.

Rules:
- Follows 44–65 accounts. Never zero — zero reads as a bot.
- Never names the app it promotes. No link in bio to it. No affiliation.
- Never a founder, never an expert, never "we". Always a peer.

> **Compliance warning — read before running this.** A persona that "never names the
> app… no affiliation" and promotes it is undisclosed promotion by a fake peer. That is
> against the spirit of App Review 5.6 (developer code of conduct), against the ad-disclosure
> rules of TikTok and Instagram, and in the EU and UK against consumer-protection law. Run it
> disclosed (a plainly Loxa-owned account, or a paid partnership label) or not at all. The
> persona faces (`hf_*.jpg`) are AI-generated and would have to be labelled as such.

**Loxa's version:** `@hair.tryon.hoarder` — *"changed my hair 40 times before
cutting it once"*. Same shape: flaw, labour, no product.

## 2. The hook formula

```
[number] + [category] + that [transgressive adverb] [outcome]
```

Harvested verbatim from their grids:

- 3 apps you need to **actually** be productive
- 4 apps that will make you **DISGUSTINGLY** productive
- hobbies that will make you **dangerously** intelligent
- 3 apps to become the most intelligent version of yourself
- how to become the best version of yourself **pt. 3**
- 10 intellectual hobbies to **unrot your brain**
- underrated glow up tips **no one talks about**
- weird habits I've **quietly** built that **actually** work
- ways to start **actually living instead of just existing**
- 3 apps I use to reach flow state
- 3 chic girl productivity apps you actually need
- fix your sleep

Three things do the work:

**The adverb.** `disgustingly`, `dangerously`, `quietly`, `ridiculously`,
`weirdly`. A mismatched adverb against a wholesome outcome. "Productive" is
boring; "disgustingly productive" is a claim you have to look at.

**The word "actually".** In roughly half of all titles. It pre-empts the "does
this work" objection *inside the hook*, before the reader has finished forming
it. Never delete it to tighten a line.

**`pt. 2` / `pt. 3`.** Turns a post into a series and a series into a reason to
follow. Costs one suffix.

Never in a hook: the app name, a CTA, a question mark, a capital letter at the
start, an emoji.

### Ported to Loxa

- 3 apps that will make you **dangerously** good at picking a haircut
- apps you need **before** you actually cut your hair
- how to know if you'd suit a bob **without cutting it**
- underrated hair tips **no one talks about**
- weird hair habits I've **quietly** built that **actually** work
- 3 apps I use to stop panic-cutting my hair
- how to become the best version of yourself (hair edition) **pt. 2**
- ways to start **actually liking** your hair instead of tolerating it
- 3 chic girl apps you actually need before a salon appointment
- things I wish I knew before I **ruined** my hair

## 3. The slide formula

**Slide 1 — the hook.** Aesthetic lifestyle photo. White centered sans,
lowercase, one to three lines, vertically centered over the image. No app
name, no logo, no CTA, no arrow, no "swipe".

**Slides 2..N — one item per slide.** Same visual language throughout.

```
[Name]                    ← bold, one line, title case, larger
[description]             ← 3–4 lines, lowercase, sentence case, smaller
```

Verbatim from their decks:

> **Albo**
> turns all the tiktok you save into organized to-do lists, so the advice you
> collect actually gets used

> **Freedom**
> blocks distracting websites and apps across devices. Excellent if you struggle
> with social media or doomscrolling.

The description formula:

```
[verb] [the mundane thing], so [the outcome you already wanted]
```

Mechanism first, benefit second, joined by "so". No adjectives about the app
itself — never "beautiful", "powerful", "AI-powered". The app does a boring
verb; the *reader* gets the outcome.

**Loxa's line:**

> **Loxa**
> puts the actual haircut on your own face before you commit, so you stop
> guessing from pinterest screenshots

### Placement

The target app sits at **position 3 of 5**. Never first — first reads as an ad.
Never last — last reads as the punchline. Third is where a reader's guard is
lowest, flanked by four real apps that solve real adjacent problems and compete
with nothing.

## 4. The visual formula

The photos are the format's whole camouflage. Get these wrong and nothing else
matters.

- Moody, low-key, desaturated. Never bright, never glam, never smiling.
- Home clothes: grey tank, oversized tee, knit sweater, silk robe. Not an outfit.
- Locations: unmade bed, sofa at night, candlelit bathroom, floor by a window,
  a doorway, a bath. Places with no event happening in them.
- Face is often turned away, looking down, or wearing a flat unsmiling stare.
- Shot on a phone. Sensor grain, imperfect framing, no retouching.

**Type set:** plain white system sans, centered, lowercase, no shadow, no box,
no outline. Exactly what iOS Photos and the Instagram story text tool produce.

**Never appears on a slide:** logo, brand colour, brand font, rounded card, app
screenshot, device frame, watermark, handle, drop shadow, gradient.

Any polish reads as an ad and the post dies. The absence of design *is* the
design.

## 5. The distribution formula

### Search anchors — the actual mechanism

Every post carries a pinned search anchor at the bottom. Theirs, verbatim:

```
Search · albo app review
Search · albo app habit tracker
Search · Freedom app
Search · what actually made todays good journal
```

They are seeding the query they want run. The post does not convert — the post
plants a **search**, and the search converts later, at the moment the viewer has
intent. That is why this beats a link in bio, which asks for the click now.

Loxa's anchors, one per post, rotated:

```
Search · loxa app
Search · loxa hair try on
Search · app that shows a haircut on your face
Search · loxa app review
```

An anchor only pays if it lands. Three things must line up or the whole machine
leaks:

1. `apps/web` ranks for those exact strings
2. the App Store listing keywords carry the same strings
3. each persona gets its own short link to the web page, and that page's App
   Store button carries a distinct `pt`/`ct` campaign token

That token pair is the **only** attribution available. TikTok passes nothing
through a search anchor — no referrer, no UTM, nothing.

### Hashtags

Four to five. Evergreen, never trending. Theirs:

`#productive #SelfImprovement #GlowUp #wellness #SelfCare #organized
#goodhabits #habits #quietlife #journal #betterself`

Loxa's: `#hair #haircut #GlowUp #hairtok #beforeandafter #hairinspo #SelfCare`

Trending tags buy a day. Evergreen tags keep the post surfacing in search for
months, which is where a slideshow earns out.

### Sound

One calm trending track, reused across every post. Theirs was the same one all
week. The sound is not doing creative work — it is doing eligibility work.

## 6. The numbers

Measured off their posts:

| likes | saves | shares | comments | save rate |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10.6K | 4,496 | 504 | 14 | 42% |
| 49.3K | 12.9K | 1,260 | 42 | 26% |
| 69.7K | 23K | 2,059 | 61 | 33% |

**Saves are 26–42% of likes. Comments are ~0.1%.**

This format is optimised for the **save**, and only the save. A list of five
apps is something you come back to; it is not something you argue with. Judging
one of these posts by its comment count is reading the wrong instrument.

Play counts inside a single account's grid run from **272 to 1M**. Roughly one
post in forty breaks out. The distribution is a power law, which means:

- never judge a single post
- judge a cohort of 40
- **kill/scale line: save rate above 20%**

Volume is the mechanism. Craft raises the ceiling; it does not move the median.

## 7. The mix

| type | what it is | share |
|---|---|---|
| **A — app list** | 5 slides, Loxa at position 3, flanked by four real non-competing apps | 50% |
| **B — transformation** | pure Loxa. Hook slide, then the same face across different cuts, straight from the real `generate-previews` renders | 25% |
| **C — tips, no apps** | pure value, zero mentions, builds the account's credibility | 25% |

C is what stops the grid reading as a shill farm. It is the cost of A working.

**Cadence:** 3 accounts × 3 posts/day × 5–8 slides ≈ 180 slides/week. Only
survivable as a script. That is the argument for `tools/ugc-slides/`, not
someone in Canva.

## 8. Where the pictures come from

Albo has to buy stock photography. Loxa **manufactures** its own. That is the
whole cost advantage, and it is worth being precise about the order to reach
for things.

### First: the bucket you already paid for

`tools/generate-previews` renders **24 cuts × 10 colours × 2 models = 480
images**, already sitting in `loxa-assets`. Every one is legal, on-topic in a
way no stock photo can be — it is literally hair — and costs nothing more to
use. For Type B decks the product's own output *is* the content.

`design-system/models/` adds 19 base busts already in the repo.

Marginal cost: **zero**. Reach for this before anything else.

### Second: the persona

The photos in this folder. One consistent face for hook slides, generated on
demand.

Consistency anchors across every render, in priority order:

1. the face and bone structure
2. long dark wavy hair
3. warm olive skin tone
4. stacked gold bangles, left wrist

The pink satin dress and yellow hoops from the first batch are **glam-set only**
— too styled for this format, which needs home clothes. Keep the bangles as the
recall anchor across the grid.

**Do not generate one photo per slide.** The reference accounts recycle a
handful of photos across dozens of posts — same bed, same couch, different text.
Budget on the order of 30 fresh persona photos a month, not 780.

#### Prompt rules, learned by getting them wrong

- **Name a concrete garment in every prompt.** With wardrobe unspecified the
  Soul defaults to bare shoulders and extrapolates to topless — the training
  images were all strapless, and it reads that as part of the identity. "wearing
  an oversized grey t-shirt" is the fix.
- **Never phrase wardrobe as a negation.** "no bare chest, nothing revealing"
  makes it *worse*: the model latches onto the noun and drops the negation. Only
  positive garment naming works.
- **Never use one blanket wardrobe clause for the whole batch.** A generic
  "t-shirt, jumper, robe or dress" collapses to the same white tank and robe in
  all fifty shots, including in a car and on a street. Wardrobe belongs in the
  per-scene line.
- **Do not pass `image_references` alongside `custom_reference_id`.** The
  reference image overrides the prompt outright and you get a near-copy of the
  reference photo, scene and hair ignored.
- **The `nsfw` filter rejects "in a bath".** Reword to clothed and beside the
  tub; the job fails with `status "nsfw"` and still costs a round trip.

### Third: generated filler

Slides that need a room and no face — candles, an unmade bed, a mirror, a
hairbrush on a sink, sunlight on a floor. Generate these rather than sourcing
them, for a non-obvious reason: **a stock photo is reverse-image-searchable.**
One viewer pasting a slide into Google and landing on an Unsplash page is the
tell that turns the post from "a girl's camera roll" into "a brand's ad". A
generated room has no match anywhere.

### Costs, measured on the Higgsfield CLI (Sept 2026)

| model | credits per image |
|---|---|
| `text2image_soul_v2` (Soul 2.0) | **0.12** |
| `nano_banana_flash` | 1.5 |
| `nano_banana_pro` | 2 |
| `seedream_v5_pro` | 3 |

Soul 2.0 is **16× cheaper** than Nano Banana Pro. That is the argument for
training a Soul reference — not convenience, cost. Training a Soul 2.0 from
10 images is a **one-off 25 credits**, charged upfront at submission. The
persona Soul is `loxa-persona` / `b4db465f-3e3f-4574-8504-a1c2213301e6`.

At 30 fresh photos a month: 60 credits without the Soul, **3.6 credits** with it.

**What the Soul cannot do: change the hair.** It learned long dark wavy hair as
part of the identity. Ask it for honey blonde or caramel and it returns a
different woman — lighter skin, different bone structure, different eyes. Jet
black on a high ponytail comes back blue. So the Soul owns the *persona*, and
**cut × colour belongs to `tools/generate-previews`**, which conditions on a real
photograph and is correct by construction. Do not try to make one tool do both.

Verify a price before relying on it — `higgsfield generate cost <model>
--prompt "test"` returns the live number, and `higgsfield account transactions`
shows what was actually charged.

### Fourth: real free stock

Only if the first three do not cover it.

| source | licence | commercial | attribution |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Unsplash** | Unsplash License | yes | not required |
| **Pexels** | Pexels License | yes | not required |
| **Pixabay** | Pixabay Content License | yes | not required |
| Openverse | mixed CC — check each image | varies | usually yes |

Use these for **objects and rooms, never people**. A recognisable face in a
stock photo carries publicity rights that the photo licence does not cover, and
none of the three grant the right to imply that person endorses Loxa — which is
exactly what a slide with a face and a hook does.

### Never

Pinterest, Google Images, TikTok screenshots, or anything scraped from the
reference accounts. Pinterest is an index of other people's copyrighted work,
not a source. Ripping the competitor slides is both infringement and instantly
recognisable to the very audience being targeted.

### Killing the stock tell

Any photo that did not come off a phone has to stop looking chosen. Four passes,
applied in the `tools/ugc-slides/` renderer to **every** background regardless
of source, so generated and stock images come out looking like the same camera
roll:

1. crop off-centre and slightly crooked — stock is perfectly composed, phones are not
2. drop saturation ~15% and lift blacks slightly — kill the HDR punch
3. add fine luminance grain
4. underexpose ~⅓ stop

Four lines of ImageMagick in the render step.

## 9. The carve-out

`CLAUDE.md` non-negotiable #3 says `design-system/` is the source of truth for
every pixel and every word of UI copy. **UGC slides are exempt, on purpose.**

They must use white system sans, no Loxa tokens, no logo, no brand colour, no
radius. A slide that matches the app's design system is a slide that reads as an
ad, and an ad does not get saved.

`design-system/` governs the app and the marketing site. This folder governs the
slides. Anyone "fixing" these to match the brand is breaking them.

## 10. Two things to settle before scaling

**Material connection.** An account owned by Loxa recommending Loxa inside a
"5 apps I use" list is an endorsement with an undisclosed material connection.
FTC endorsement guides cover it; Albo is running the same exposure. Disclosing
kills Type A's mechanism outright. Types B and C are clean — B is transparently
a product demo, C mentions nothing. Choose knowingly: B+C only, or A with the
risk understood.

**AI labelling.** TikTok labels AI-generated imagery, and a labelled slideshow
reads as an ad — the one thing this format cannot survive being. Test the
persona renders in this folder against TikTok's detector on a burner account
*before* committing to 180 slides a week.
